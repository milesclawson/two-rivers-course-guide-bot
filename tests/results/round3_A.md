## R1: Which is better for college credit, AP or concurrent enrollment?

Neither is better for everyone. The guide says there is no single better choice, since it depends on you and the colleges you're considering.

- **AP:** Credit depends on one exam in May. A 3 or higher gives you a chance at credit, but each college sets its own rules. The class is free, but the exam has a fee (waivers may be available). A low score doesn't create a college GPA.
- **Concurrent Enrollment (CE/CIS):** Credit depends on your course grade. It goes on a real college transcript and starts a college GPA, so a poor grade can hurt. These courses are generally free. CE tends to be most reliable at in-state public colleges and the partner college.
- **PSEO:** This is a separate state program. You take classes on a college campus or online, tuition-free, but it means a heavier schedule and travel.

Quick tip: AP is often more widely accepted at selective and out-of-state colleges. No college is guaranteed to take either one, so check the college's website or registrar. Both are a lot of work, usually 1-2 hours outside class per class period.

## R4: Can I do PSEO instead of taking classes at Two Rivers?

Possibly for some classes. Here is what the guide says about PSEO:
- It allows qualified sophomores, juniors and seniors to take college courses on Minnesota college campuses.
- The classes are taught by college instructors, on campus or online, and you follow the college's schedule.
- The college decides if you're eligible.
- It's free to students. You earn high school credit and a weighted high school GPA, and the grade goes on your college transcript.
- The handout adds that PSEO means rearranging your schedule and traveling, which can be hard if you're busy with activities.

Timing matters. The guide says you must tell your counselor in writing by October 30 or May 30 each year that you plan to enroll in college courses for the next term, and you're bound by whether you notify or not. October 30 has already passed this year, so please talk to your counselor soon about what that means for you.

## R5: Will taking CE classes hurt my GPA or my scholarships?

It can, but it doesn't have to. Here is what the guide says.

**GPA**
- CE grades count in your high school GPA. Many CE courses are weighted x1.2, per the course descriptions.
- The grade also goes on a real college transcript and starts a college GPA. A poor grade there is permanent and can affect your academic standing and scholarship eligibility.

**Scholarships**
- Some universities give merit scholarships only to first-time freshmen with fewer than a set number of transfer credits (for example, under 12-15). Too many credits could disqualify you. Always check each college's scholarship terms.
- Lots of credit can also mean having to declare a major earlier (the guide mentions at 60 credits).

**Other things to know**
- The guide says CE credit needs "a D or better" in one place and "a C or better" in another. The partner college sets the exact rule, so ask your counselor.
- College-level classes usually mean 1-2 hours of work outside class per class period.

Do you have colleges in mind? Checking their scholarship and transfer-credit rules is a smart next step.

## R6: How do I apply for TriDistrict CAPS?

Here's how it works, based on the 2026-27 guide and the CAPS website (careercollegeready.org):

1. **Check eligibility.** CAPS is for juniors and seniors who are on track to graduate. The guide also lists good attendance and behavior.
2. **Register for the CAPS course** at Two Rivers during the regular registration in December.
3. **Submit the online application.** Only online applications are accepted. It takes about 15-20 minutes and includes a recommendation from a teacher, coach, counselor or administrator. The guide says applications are due at the close of registration.
4. **Read the Student & Family Program Guide** before you apply, because applying means agreeing to its rules.

Seats are limited (about 10-15 per program per school), so it's expected to be competitive. Some placements also involve an internship interview.

The CAPS website still shows some older dates, so please confirm the details with your counselor. You can also contact the CAPS director, Ben Kusch, at bkusch@tridistrictce.org or 612-267-2760.

## R7: I'm a sophomore. Can I do CAPS Healthcare next year?

Probably yes on grade level. Per the 2026-27 guide, TriDistrict CAPS Healthcare & Medicine (4013/4014) is for grades 11-12. Application requirements list "rising junior or senior who is on track to graduate", so a sophomore now would be a junior next year.

Here are the details:
- Prerequisite: none, but an application is required.
- Full year, 3 credits (2 elective and 1 English). Homework Guide is 2 hours a week.
- It meets periods 5-7 at the South St. Paul Community Learning Center, and the guide says transportation is provided.
- The first half is learning from healthcare professionals. The second half is a competitive internship.
- Also needed: good attendance and behavior, and a recommendation with your application.

Seats are limited, so it isn't guaranteed. Registration is in December, and the application is due at the close of registration. Next year's guide may differ, so confirm with your counselor.
