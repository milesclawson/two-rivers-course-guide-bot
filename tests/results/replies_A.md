## Q1: What do I need before I can take PreAP Algebra II with Trig?

Per the 2026-27 guide, PreAP Algebra II with Trigonometry (0431/0432) lists this prerequisite:

- A grade of "B+" or better in Intermediate Algebra and PreAP Geometry, and/or teacher recommendation.

A few other things to know:
- It's a full-year, 1-credit course open to grades 9-12.
- The Homework Guide is 4 hours a week, so it's a heavier load than regular Algebra II (0421/0422), which is 3 hours.
- The guide suggests a TI-83 or TI-84 graphing calculator.
- It leads to AP Pre Calculus (0473/0474). The guide says you need PreAP Algebra II with Trig before AP Pre Calculus.

So you'd want Intermediate Algebra (0411/0412) and PreAP Geometry (0443/0444) done first, or talk with your teacher about a recommendation. Your counselor can help you check this against your own record.

## Q2: How many credits do I need to graduate?

Good news: the total is 23 credits for the Class of 2027, the Class of 2028, and the Class of 2029 and beyond, per the 2026-27 guide. What changes is how those 23 credits are split up.

- Class of 2027: Social Studies is 3.5 credits, Science includes Physical Science or Pre-AP Science, and you have 6.5 elective credits. There is no separate Personal Finance credit.
- Class of 2028: Social Studies is 4 credits, there is a .5 credit Personal Finance requirement, and you have 5.5 elective credits. Science still includes Physical Science or Pre-AP Science.
- Class of 2029 and beyond: the same as 2028, except the 9th-grade science is Earth and Space Science or AP Environmental Science.

For all classes:
- Language Arts: 4 credits
- Math: 3 credits (you must get through Algebra II or higher)
- Science: 3 credits
- PE and Health: 1.5 credits
- Arts: 1 credit
- Warrior Seminar: .5 credit

## Q3: What's the difference between Geometry and PreAP Geometry?

Both are full-year, 1-credit courses. Here's how they compare per the 2026-27 guide:

**Geometry (0441/0442)**
- Grades 9-12
- Prerequisite: Intermediate Algebra or equivalent. Guideline: "C" or above OR teacher recommendation.
- Covers measurement formulas, three-dimensional figures, congruence, similarity, and formal reasoning, with coordinate geometry and review of algebra skills.
- Homework Guide: 3 hours a week

**PreAP Geometry (0443/0444)**
- Grades 8-10 (8th graders get in through middle-school math acceleration)
- Prerequisite: Intermediate Algebra or equivalent. Guideline: "B+" or above OR teacher recommendation.
- Rigorous and fast-paced. It extends everything in Geometry and adds data analysis, statistics, matrices, and financial literacy.
- Prepares you for PreAP Algebra II with Trigonometry, AP PreCalculus, and AP Calculus.
- Homework Guide: 3 hours a week

Both are good paths. PreAP Geometry moves faster and sets you up for the advanced math track. Geometry is a solid choice that leads to Algebra II. If you're wondering which fits you, talk with your math teacher and counselor, since a teacher recommendation can matter.

## Q4: Does AP Calculus count for college credit?

It can, but it depends on the college. Per the 2026-27 guide, students who successfully complete AP Calculus AB (0481/0482) and the AP exam "may receive college credit, advanced placement, or both," according to each college's own policy. The guide says the same for AP Calculus BC (0483/0484). In both courses you're expected to take the AP exam.

Good to know:
- AP Calculus AB prerequisite: PreAP Algebra II with Trigonometry AND AP Pre-Calculus. Guideline: "C" or better in PreAP Algebra II w/ Trig AND "C+" or better in AP Pre-Calculus.
- AP Calculus BC prerequisite: AP Calculus (AB), "C" or better or teacher recommendation.
- Both are weighted (1.2 multiplier) and have a Homework Guide of 6 hours a week.

The guide doesn't say which specific colleges accept AP scores or how many credits they give. Your counselor or the college you're interested in can tell you.

## Q5: How does the weighted GPA work?

Per the 2026-27 guide, grades are weighted for courses offered at Two Rivers that are tied to college credit and for PSEO courses. These courses are marked in their descriptions as "Weighted x1.2" (a 1.2 multiplier).

Here's the difference for a few grades:

| Grade | Regular (4.0 scale) | Weighted |
|---|---|---|
| A | 4.0 | 4.8 |
| A- | 3.67 | 4.4 |
| B+ | 3.33 | 4.0 |
| B | 3.0 | 3.6 |
| B- | 2.67 | 3.2 |
| C | 2.0 | 2.4 |
| D | 1.0 | 1.2 |
| F | 0.0 | 0.0 |

(The guide also lists the C+, C-, D+, and D- values. Just ask if you want them.)

So a B in a weighted course counts as a 3.6 instead of a 3.0. An F is still 0.0. Also, credits are earned for grades at or above a D-.

The guide doesn't explain how the school figures out class rank or any other GPA details beyond this. Your counselor can help with that.

## Q7: I want to drop a class in October, what happens?

Per the 2026-27 guide, here's how course drops work:
- If you drop a course between weeks 3-10 of the semester, you get an "NG" (No Grade).
- If you drop after one quarter, you get an "F" for the semester.

The guide doesn't tell me the exact calendar dates for weeks 3-10 or the end of the first quarter, so I can't say for sure which one October falls into. The main office (651-403-7100) or your counselor can tell you the date.

There are a few other rules:
- 9th and 10th graders who already have a study hall must finish the semester in the course.
- 11th and 12th graders must be on track for graduation to drop a course.
- If you chose to "try out" a course, you can't drop it until the end of the semester.

Also, if the class you drop is a required one, you'll still have to make up that credit to graduate. Since a drop can affect your record and your graduation plan, please talk with your counselor before you decide. The Counseling Office is 651-403-7208, and counselors are assigned by last name.
