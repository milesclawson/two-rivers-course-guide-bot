## R8: Do concurrent enrollment credits transfer to Harvard?

I can't tell you that, and no one can promise it. The guide I use doesn't list Harvard's credit policy, and it says no credit transfer is guaranteed: the college you're going to has the final say.

Here is what the guide does say:
- CE credit is guaranteed by the partner college (like Inver Hills or Mankato) once you pass. It transfers most easily inside Minnesota's public system. Other schools may review the course before deciding.
- Private and out-of-state schools can be pickier. The guide also says AP credit is generally more widely accepted by highly selective and out-of-state colleges.
- Colleges may give credit for a specific course, elective credit only, or no credit at all (placement only).

2. Try Transferology, which the guide mentions for checking how courses "match."
3. Save every course syllabus. You can use it to ask for credit later.
4. After final grades post, have the partner college send an official transcript straight to the school.

Policies change, so please confirm with the college and your counselor (Counseling Office, 651-403-7208).

## R9: I'm a freshman. Should I do AP Human Geo plus Enrichment, or AVID 9?

Good news: per the 2026-27 guide, these aren't strictly either/or. Every 9th grader needs one transition course: Warrior Seminar, AVID 9, or AP Human Geo Enrichment. The 9th-grade worksheet says students in AVID 9 and AP Human Geo can also take Enrichment if they want.

**AP Human Geography (0287/0288) + AP Human Geo Enrichment (0093/0094)**
- AP Human Geo: full year, Homework Guide 5 hrs/week, weighted grade, prepares you for the May AP exam.
- Enrichment: full year, 2 hrs/week, grade 9 only. You must also be in AP Human Geo. It replaces Warrior Seminar.

**AVID 9 (AVID I, 0641/0642)**
- Full year, Homework Guide 1 hr/week, application and interview required.
- Prerequisite: Intermediate Algebra or higher plus at least one advanced, AP, or Pre-AP course.
- Focus: note-taking, writing, reading, and test-taking skills.

Think about your total workload. The guide says to plan on at least 1.5 hours of homework a night, and advanced classes need more. Also, registration is annual in December and considered final, and you have to list alternates.

Since you're already a freshman, are you asking about this year's schedule or planning for next year? Your counselor can tell you what's possible.

## R12: Is it a good idea to take as many college-credit classes as I can so I can graduate college early?

It can be a good fit for some students, but it isn't the right plan for everyone. The guide lists both sides.

**Possible upsides**
- Skip intro classes and save time and maybe tuition.
- Room to double major, minor, or study abroad, or finish early.
- Confidence that you can handle college work.

**Things to watch for**
- Some merit scholarships go only to freshmen with fewer than a set number of transfer credits (for example under 12-15). Check scholarship rules.
- Too many credits may mean declaring a major at 60 credits.
- CE grades go on a real college transcript and start your college GPA, so a poor grade can hurt.
- AP credit depends on one May exam, and no college is required to accept it.
- Expect 1-2 hours of work outside class per class period. That can crowd out activities and sleep.
- You might arrive at college as a sophomore or junior, which can feel different socially.

Both paths, rigorous and not, are valid. The guide suggests picking courses you're interested in that fit your whole schedule.

## R14: I'm an 8th grader, what math should I be in?

It depends on the math you're in now, and the guide doesn't say how middle schools place students. Per the 2026-27 guide, here's the high school side:
- Students on the standard path start earning high school math credit in 9th grade with **Intermediate Algebra (0411/0412)**, Homework Guide 2.
- Students who accelerated in middle school can start earlier. Intermediate Algebra opens at grade 7, and **PreAP Geometry (0443/0444)** is open to 8th graders through acceleration.
- The 9th-grade worksheet says: if you've had Intermediate Algebra, choose **Geometry (0441/0442)** or PreAP Geometry. If you've had Geometry, choose Algebra 2 with Trig or Algebra 2.

Guidelines as written:
- Geometry: "C" or above OR teacher recommendation. Homework 3 hrs/week.
- PreAP Geometry: "B+" or above OR teacher recommendation. Homework 3 hrs/week.

Everyone needs 3 math credits through at least Algebra II. Faster or standard are both valid paths.
